# John 6 (Weymouth's New Testament)

[[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/43 - John/John 5|← John 5]] | [[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/43 - John/John]] | [[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/43 - John/John 7|John 7 →]]

***

v1 After this Jesus went away across the Lake of Galilee (that is, the Lake of Tiberias).

v2 A vast multitude followed Him, because they witnessed the miracles on the sick which He was constantly performing.

v3 Then Jesus went up the hill, and sat there with His disciples.

v4 The Jewish Festival, the Passover, was at hand.

v5 And when He looked round and saw an immense crowd coming towards Him, He said to Philip, "Where shall we buy bread for all these people to eat?"

v6 He said this to put Philip to the test, for He Himself knew what He was going to do.

v7 "Seven pounds' worth of bread," replied Philip, "is not enough for them all to get even a scanty meal."

v8 One of His disciples, Andrew, Simon Peter's brother, said to Him,

v9 "There is a boy here with five barley loaves and a couple of fish: but what is that among so many?"

v10 "Make the people sit down," said Jesus. The ground was covered with thick grass; so they sat down, the adult men numbering about 5,000.

v11 Then Jesus took the loaves, and after giving thanks He distributed them to those who were resting on the ground; and also the fish in like manner--as much as they desired.

v12 When all were fully satisfied, He said to His disciples, "Gather up the broken portions that remain over, so that nothing be lost."

v13 Accordingly they gathered them up; and with the fragments of the five barley loaves--the broken portions that remained over after they had done eating--they filled twelve baskets.

v14 Thereupon the people, having seen the miracle He had performed, said, "This is indeed the Prophet who was to come into the world."

v15 Perceiving, however, that they were about to come and carry Him off by force to make Him a king, Jesus withdrew again up the hill alone by Himself.

v16 When evening came on, His disciples went down to the Lake.

v17 There they got on board a boat, and pushed off to cross the Lake to Capernaum. By this time it had become dark, and Jesus had not yet joined them.

v18 The Lake also was getting rough, because a strong wind was blowing.

v19 When, however, they had rowed three or four miles, they saw Jesus walking on the water and coming near the boat.

v20 They were terrified; but He called to them. "It is I," He said, "do not be afraid."

v21 Then they were willing to take Him on board; and in a moment the boat reached the shore at the point to which they were going.

v22 Next morning the crowd who were still standing about on the other side of the Lake found that there had been but one small boat there, and they had seen that Jesus did not go on board with His disciples, but that His disciples went away without Him.

v23 Yet a number of small boats came from Tiberias to the neighbourhood of the place where they had eaten the bread after the Lord had given thanks.

v24 When however the crowd saw that neither Jesus nor His disciples were there, they themselves also took boats and came to Capernaum to look for Jesus.

v25 So when they had crossed the Lake and had found Him, they asked Him, "Rabbi, when did you come here?"

v26 "In most solemn truth I tell you," replied Jesus, "that you are searching for me not because you have seen miracles, but because you ate the loaves and had a hearty meal.

v27 Bestow your pains not on the food which perishes, but on the food that remains unto the Life of the Ages--that food which will be the Son of Man's gift to you; for on Him the Father, God, has set His seal."

v28 "What are we to do," they asked, "in order to carry out the things that God requires?"

v29 "This," replied Jesus, "is above all the thing that God requires--that you should be believers in Him whom He has sent."

v30 "What miracle then," they asked, "do you perform for us to see and become believers in you? What do you *do*?

v31 Our forefathers ate the manna in the Desert, as it is written, <'He gave them bread out of Heaven to eat'.">

v32 "In most solemn truth I tell you," replied Jesus, "that Moses did not give you the bread out of Heaven, but my Father is giving you the bread--the true bread--out of Heaven.

v33 For God's bread is that which comes down out of Heaven and gives Life to the world."

v34 "Sir," they said, "always give us that bread."

v35 "I am the bread of Life," replied Jesus; "he who comes to me shall never hunger, and he who believes in me shall never, never thirst.

v36 But it is as I have said to you: you have seen me and yet you do not believe.

v37 Every one whom the Father gives me will come to me, and him who comes to me I will never on any account drive away.

v38 For I have left Heaven and have come down to earth not to seek my own pleasure, but to do the will of Him who sent me.

v39 And this is the will of Him who sent me, that of all that He has given me I should lose nothing, but should raise it to life on the last day.

v40 For this is my Father's will, that every one who fixes his gaze on the Son of God and believes in Him should have the Life of the Ages, and I will raise him to life on the last day."

v41 Now the Jews began to find fault about Him because of His claiming to be the bread which came down out of Heaven.

v42 They kept asking, "Is not this man Joseph's son? Is he not Jesus, whose father and mother we know? What does he mean by now saying, 'I have come down out of Heaven'?"

v43 "Do not thus find fault among yourselves," replied Jesus;

v44 "no one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him; then I will raise him to life on the last day.

v45 It stands written in the Prophets, <'And they shall all of them be taught by God'.> Every one who listens to the Father and learns from Him comes to me.

v46 No one has ever seen the Father--except Him who is from God. He has seen the Father.

v47 "In most solemn truth I tell you that he who believes has the Life of the Ages.

v48 I am the bread of Life.

v49 Your forefathers ate the manna in the Desert, and they died.

v50 Here is the bread that comes down out of Heaven that a man may eat it and not die.

v51 I am the living bread come down out of Heaven. If a man eats this bread, he shall live for ever. Moreover the bread which I will give is my flesh given for the life of the world."

v52 This led to an angry debate among the Jews. "How can this man," they argued, "give us his flesh to eat?"

v53 "In most solemn truth I tell you," said Jesus, "that unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood, you have no Life in you.

v54 He who eats my flesh and drinks my blood has the Life of the Ages, and I will raise him up on the last day.

v55 For my flesh is true food, and my blood is true drink.

v56 He who eats my flesh and drinks my blood remains in union with me, and I remain in union with him.

v57 As the ever-living Father has sent me, and I live because of the Father, so also he who eats me will live because of me.

v58 This is the bread which came down out of Heaven; it is unlike that which your forefathers ate--for they ate and yet died. He who eats this bread shall live for ever."

v59 Jesus said all this in the synagogue while teaching at Capernaum.

v60 Many therefore of His disciples, when they heard it, said, "This is hard to accept. Who can listen to such teaching?"

v61 But, knowing in Himself that His disciples were dissatisfied about it, Jesus asked them,

v62 "Does this seem incredible to you? What then if you were to see the Son of Man ascending again where He was before?

v63 It is the spirit which gives Life. The flesh confers no benefit whatever. The words I have spoken to you are spirit and are Life.

v64 But there are some of you who do not believe." For Jesus knew from the beginning who those were that did not believe, and who it was that would betray Him.

v65 So He added, "That is why I told you that no one can come to me unless it be granted him by the Father."

v66 Thereupon many of His disciples left Him and went away, and no longer associated with Him.

v67 Jesus therefore appealed to the Twelve. "Will you go also?" He asked.

v68 "Master," replied Simon Peter, "to whom shall we go? Your teachings tell us of the Life of the Ages.

v69 And we have come to believe and know that *you* are indeed the Holy One of God."

v70 "Did not I choose you--the Twelve?" said Jesus, "and even of you one is a devil."

v71 He alluded to Judas, the son of Simon the Iscariot. For he it was who, though one of the Twelve, was afterwards to betray Him.
