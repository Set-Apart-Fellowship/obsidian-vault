# Luke 23 (Weymouth's New Testament)

[[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/42 - Luke/Luke 22|← Luke 22]] | [[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/42 - Luke/Luke]] | [[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/42 - Luke/Luke 24|Luke 24 →]]

***

v1 Then the whole assembly rose and brought Him to Pilate, and began to accuse Him.

v2 "We have found this man," they said, "an agitator among our nation, forbook_idding the payment of tribute to Caesar, and claiming to be himself an anointed king."

v3 Then Pilate asked Him, "You, then, are the King of the Jews?" "It is as you say," He replied.

v4 Pilate said to the High Priests and to the crowd, "I can find no crime in this man."

v5 But they violently insisted. "He stirs up the people," they said, "throughout all Judaea with His teaching--even from Galilee (where He first started) to this city."

v6 On hearing this, Pilate inquired, "Is this man a Galilaean?"

v7 And learning that He belonged to Herod's jurisdiction he sent Him to Herod, for he too was in Jerusalem at that time.

v8 To Herod the sight of Jesus was a great gratification, for, for a long time, he had been wanting to see Him, because he had heard so much about Him. He hoped also to see some miracle performed by Him.

v9 So he put a number of questions to Him, but Jesus gave him no reply.

v10 Meanwhile the High Priests and the Scribes were standing there and vehemently accusing Him.

v11 Then, laughing to scorn the claims of Jesus, Herod (and his soldiers with him) made sport of Him, dressed Him in a gorgeous costume, and sent Him back to Pilate.

v12 And on that very day Herod and Pilate became friends again, for they had been for some time at enmity.

v13 Then calling together the High Priests and the Rulers and the people, Pilate said,

v14 "You have brought this man to me on a charge of corrupting the loyalty of the people. But, you see, I have examined him in your presence and have discovered in the man no ground for the accusations which you bring against him.

v15 No, nor does Herod; for he has sent him back to us; and, you see, there is nothing he has done that deserves death.

v16 I will therefore give him a light punishment and release him."

v17 []

v18 Then the whole multitude burst out into a shout. "Away with this man," they said, "and release Barabbas to us"

v19 --Barabbas! who had been lodged in jail for some time in connexion with a riot which had occurred in the city, and for murder.

v20 But Pilate once more addressed them, wishing to set Jesus free.

v21 They, however, persistently shouted, "Crucify, crucify him!"

v22 A third time he appealed to them: "Why, what crime has the man committed? I have discovered in him nothing that deserves death. I will therefore give him a light punishment and release him."

v23 But they urgently insisted, demanding with frantic outcries that He should be crucified; and their clamour prevailed.

v24 So Pilate gave judgement, yielding to their demand.

v25 The man who was lying in prison charged with riot and murder and for whom they clamoured he set free, but Jesus he gave up to be dealt with as they desired.

v26 As soon as they led Him away, they laid hold on one Simon, a Cyrenaean, who was coming in from the country, and on his shoulders they put the cross, for him to carry it behind Jesus.

v27 A vast crowd of the people also followed Him, and of women who were beating their breasts and wailing for Him.

v28 But Jesus turned towards them and said, "Daughters of Jerusalem, weep not for me, but weep for yourselves and for your children.

v29 For a time is coming when they will say, 'Blessed are the women who never bore children, and the breasts which have never given nourishment.'

v30 Then will they begin to say to the mountains, 'Fall on us;' and to the hills, 'Cover us.'

v31 For if they are doing these things in the case of the green tree, what will be done in that of the dry?"

v32 They brought also two others, criminals, to put them to death with Him.

v33 When they reached the place called 'The Skull,' there they nailed Him to the cross, and the criminals also, one at His right hand and one at His left.

v34 Jesus prayed, "Father, forgive them, for they know not what they are doing." And they divided His garments among them, drawing lots for them;

v35 and the people stood looking on. The Rulers, too, repeatedly uttered their bitter taunts. "This fellow," they said, "saved others: let him save himself, if he is God's Anointed, the Chosen One."

v36 And the soldiers also made sport of Him, coming and offering Him sour wine and saying,

v37 "Are *you* the King of the Jews? Save yourself, then!"

v38 There was moreover a writing over His head: THIS IS THE KING OF THE JEWS.

v39 Now one of the criminals who had been crucified insulted Him, saying, "Are not you the Christ? Save yourself and us."

v40 But the other, answering, reproved him. "Do you also not fear God," he said, "when you are actually suffering the same punishment?

v41 And we indeed are suffering justly, for we are receiving due requital for what we have done. But He has done nothing amiss."

v42 And he said, "Jesus, remember me when you come in your Kingdom."

v43 "I tell you in solemn truth," replied Jesus, "that this very day you shall be with me in Paradise."

v44 It was now about noon, and a darkness came over the whole country till three o'clock in the afternoon.

v45 The sun was darkened, and the curtain of the Sanctuary was torn down the middle,

v46 and Jesus cried out in a loud voice, and said, "Father, to Thy hands I entrust my spirit." And after uttering these words He yielded up His spirit.

v47 The Captain, seeing what had happened, gave glory to God, saying, "Beyond question this man was innocent."

v48 And all the crowds that had come together to this sight, after seeing all that had occurred, returned to the city beating their breasts.

v49 But all His acquaintances, and the women who had been His followers after leaving Galilee, continued standing at a distance and looking on.

v50 There was a member of the Council of the name of Joseph, a kind-hearted and upright man,

v51 who came from the Jewish town of Arimathaea and was awaiting the coming of the Kingdom of God. He had not concurred in the design or action of the Council,

v52 and now he went to Pilate and asked for the body of Jesus.

v53 Then, taking it down, he wrapped it in a linen sheet and laid it in a tomb in the rock, where no one else had yet been put.

v54 It was the Preparation Day, and the Sabbath was near at hand.

v55 The women--those who had come with Jesus from Galilee--followed close behind, and saw the tomb and how His body was placed.

v56 Then they returned, and prepared spices and perfumes. On the Sabbath they rested in obedience to the Commandment.
