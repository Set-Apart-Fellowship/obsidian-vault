# 1 Corinthians 11 (Weymouth's New Testament)

[[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10|← 1 Corinthians 10]] | [[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians]] | [[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 12|1 Corinthians 12 →]]

***

v1 Be imitators of me, in so far as I in turn am an imitator of Christ.

v2 Now I commend you for remembering me in everything, and because you hold fast truths and practices precisely as I have taught them to you.

v3 I would have you know, however, that of every man, Christ is the Head, that of a woman her husband is the Head, and that God is Christ's Head.

v4 A man who wears a veil when praying or prophesying dishonors his Head;

v5 but a woman who prays or prophesies with her head uncovered dishonors her Head, for it is exactly the same as if she had her hair cut short.

v6 If a woman will not wear a veil, let her also cut off her hair. But since it is a dishonor to a woman to have her hair cut off or her head shaved, let her wear a veil.

v7 For a man ought not to have a veil on his head, since he is the image and glory of God; while woman is the glory of man.

v8 Man does not take his origin from woman, but woman takes hers from man.

v9 For man was not created for woman's sake, but woman for man's.

v10 That is why a woman ought to have on her head a symbol of subjection, because of the angels.

v11 Yet, in the Lord, woman is not independent of man nor man independent of woman.

v12 For just as woman originates from man, so also man comes into existence through woman, but everything springs originally from God.

v13 Judge of this for your own selves: is it seemly for a woman to pray to God when she is unveiled?

v14 Does not Nature itself teach you that if a man has long hair it is a dishonor to him,

v15 but that if a woman has long hair it is her glory, because her hair was given her for a covering?

v16 But if any one is inclined to be contentious on the point, we have no such custom, nor have the Churches of God.

v17 But while giving you these instructions, there is one thing I cannot praise--your meeting together, with bad rather than good results.

v18 for, in the first place, when you meet as a Church, there are divisions among you. This is what I am told, and I believe that there is some truth in it.

v19 For there must of necessity be differences of opinion among you, in order that it may be plainly seen who are the men of sterling worth among you.

v20 When, however, you meet in one place, there is no eating the Supper of the Lord;

v21 for it is his own supper of which each of you is in a hurry to partake, and one eats like a hungry man, while another has already drunk to excess.

v22 Why, have you no homes in which to eat and drink? Or do you wish to show your contempt for the Church of God and make those who have no homes feel ashamed? What shall I say to you? Shall I praise you? In this matter I certainly do not praise you.

v23 For it was from the Lord that I received the facts which, in turn, I handed on to you; how that the Lord Jesus, on the night He was to be betrayed, took some bread,

v24 and after giving thanks He broke it and said, "This is my body which is about to be broken for you. Do this in memory of me."

v25 In the same way, when the meal was over, He also took the cup. "This cup," He said, "is the new Covenant of which my blood is the pledge. Do this, every time that you drink it, in memory of me."

v26 For every time that you eat this bread and drink from the cup, you are proclaiming the Lord's death--until He returns.

v27 Whoever, therefore, in an unworthy manner, eats the bread or drinks from the cup of the Lord sins against the body and blood of the Lord.

v28 But let a man examine himself, and, having done that, then let him eat the bread and drink from the cup.

v29 For any one who eats and drinks, eats and drinks judgement to himself, if he fails to estimate the body aright.

v30 That is why many among you are sickly and out of health, and why not a few die.

v31 If, however, we estimated ourselves aright, we should not be judged.

v32 But when we are judged by the Lord, chastisement follows, to save us from being condemned along with the world.

v33 Therefore, brethren, when you come together for this meal, wait for one another.

v34 If any one is hungry, let him eat at home; so that your coming together may not lead to judgement. The other matters I will deal with whenever I come.
