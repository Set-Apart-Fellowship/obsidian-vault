# Mark 12 (Weymouth's New Testament)

[[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/41 - Mark/Mark 11|← Mark 11]] | [[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/41 - Mark/Mark]] | [[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/41 - Mark/Mark 13|Mark 13 →]]

***

v1 Then He began to speak to them in figurative language. "There was once a man," He said, "who planted a vineyard, fenced it round, dug a pit for the wine-tank, and built a strong lodge. Then he let the place to vine-dressers and went abroad.

v2 At vintage-time he sent one of his servants to receive from the vine-dressers a share of the grapes.

v3 But they seized him, beat him cruelly and sent him away empty-handed.

v4 Again he sent to them another servant: and as for him, they wounded him in the head and treated him shamefully.

v5 Yet a third he sent, and him they killed. And he sent many besides, and them also they ill-treated, beating some and killing others.

v6 He had still one left whom he could send, a dearly-loved son: him last of all he sent, saying, "'They will treat my son with respect.'

v7 "But those men--the vine-dressers--said to one another, "'Here is the heir: come, let us kill him, and then the property will one day be ours.'

v8 "So they took him and killed him, and flung his body outside the vineyard.

v9 What, therefore, will the owner of the vineyard do?" "He will come and put the vine-dressers to death," they said; "and will give the vineyard to others."

v10 "Have you not read even this passage," He added, <"'The stone which the builders rejected has become the Cornerstone:

v11 this Cornerstone came from the Lord, and is wonderful in our esteem?'">

v12 And they kept looking out for an opportunity to seize Him, but were afraid of the people; for they saw that in this parable He had referred to *them*. So they left Him and went away.

v13 Their next step was to send to Him some of the Pharisees and of Herod's partisans to entrap Him in conversation.

v14 So they came to Him. "Rabbi," they said, "we know that you are a truthful man and you do not fear any one; for you do not recognize human distinctions, but teach God's way truly. Is it allowable to pay poll-tax to Caesar, or not?

v15 Shall we pay, or shall we refuse to pay?" But He, knowing their hypocrisy, replied, "Why try to ensnare me? Bring me a shilling for me to look at."

v16 They brought one; and He asked them, "Whose is this likeness and this inscription?" "Caesar's," they replied.

v17 "What is Caesar's," replied Jesus, "pay to Caesar--and what is God's, pay to God." And they wondered exceedingly at Him.

v18 Then came to Him a party of Sadducees, a sect which denies that there is any Resurrection; and they proceeded to question Him.

v19 "Rabbi," they said, "Moses made it a law for us: <'If a man's brother should die and leave a wife, but no child, the man shall marry the widow and raise up a family for his brother.'>

v20 There were once seven brothers, the eldest of whom married a wife, but at his death left no family.

v21 The second married her, and died, leaving no family; and the third did the same.

v22 And so did the rest of the seven, all dying childless. Finally the woman also died.

v23 At the Resurrection whose wife will she be? For they all seven married her."

v24 "Is not this the cause of your error," replied Jesus--"your ignorance alike of the Scriptures and of the power of God?

v25 For when they have risen from among the dead, men do not marry and women are not given in marriage, but they are as angels are in Heaven.

v26 But as to the dead, that they rise to life, have you never read in the Book of Moses, in the passage about the Bush, how God said to him, <'I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob?'>

v27 He is not the God of dead, but of living men. You are in grave error."

v28 Then one of the Scribes, who had heard them disputing and well knew that Jesus had given them an answer to the point, and a forcible one, came forward and asked Him, "Which is the chief of all the Commandments?"

v29 "The chief Commandment," replied Jesus, "is this: <'Hear, O Israel! The Lord our God is one Lord;

v30 and thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart, thy whole soul, thy whole mind, and thy whole strength.'>

v31 "The second is this: <'Thou shalt love thy fellow man as thou lovest thyself.'> "Other Commandment greater than these there is none."

v32 So the Scribe said to Him, "Rightly, in very truth, Rabbi, have you said that <He stands alone, and there is none but He;>

v33 and <To love Him with all one's heart, with all one's understanding, and with all one's strength, and to love one's fellow man no less than oneself,> is far better than all our <whole burnt-offerings and sacrifices.">

v34 Perceiving that the Scribe had answered wisely Jesus said to him, "You are not far from the Kingdom of God." No one from that time forward ventured to put any question to Him.

v35 But, while teaching in the Temple, Jesus asked, "How is it the Scribes say that the Christ is a son of David?

v36 David himself said, taught by the Holy Spirit, <"'The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at My right hand, until I have made thy foes a footstool under thy feet.'>

v37 "David himself calls Him 'Lord:' how then can He be his son?" And the mass of people found pleasure in listening to Jesus.

v38 Moreover in the course of His teaching He said, "Be on your guard against the Scribes who like to walk about in long robes and to be bowed to in places of public resort,

v39 and to occupy the best seats in the synagogues and at dinner parties,

v40 and who swallow up the property of widows and then mask their wickedness by making long prayers: these men will receive far heavier punishment."

v41 Having taken a seat opposite the Treasury, He observed how the people were dropping money into the Treasury, and that many of the wealthy threw in large sums.

v42 But there came one poor widow and dropped in two farthings, equal in value to a halfpenny.

v43 So He called His disciples to Him and said, "In solemn truth I tell you that this widow, poor as she is, has thrown in more than all the other contributors to the Treasury;

v44 for they have all contributed out of what they could well spare, but she out of her need has thrown in all she possessed--all she had to live on."
