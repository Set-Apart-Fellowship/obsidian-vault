# Mark 9 (Weymouth's New Testament)

[[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/41 - Mark/Mark 8|← Mark 8]] | [[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/41 - Mark/Mark]] | [[Bible Versions/WEYMOUTH/41 - Mark/Mark 10|Mark 10 →]]

***

v1 He went on to say, "In solemn truth I tell you that some of those who are standing here will certainly not taste death till they have seen the Kingdom of God already come in power."

v2 Six days later, Jesus took with Him Peter, James, and John, and brought them alone, apart from the rest, up a high mountain; and in their presence His appearance underwent a change.

v3 His garments also became dazzling with brilliant whiteness--such whiteness as no bleaching on earth could give.

v4 Moreover there appeared to them Elijah accompanied by Moses; and the two were conversing with Jesus,

v5 when Peter said to Jesus, "Rabbi, we are thankful to you that we are here. Let us put up three tents--one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah."

v6 For he knew not what to say: they were filled with such awe.

v7 Then there came a cloud spreading over them, and a voice issued from the cloud, "This is my Son, dearly loved: listen to Him."

v8 Instantly they looked round, and now they could no longer see any one, but themselves and Jesus.

v9 As they were coming down from the mountain, He very strictly forbad them to tell any one what they had seen "until after the Son of Man has risen from among the dead."

v10 So they kept the matter to themselves, although frequently asking one another what was meant by the rising from the dead.

v11 They also asked Him, "How is it that the Scribes say that Elijah must first come?"

v12 "Elijah," He replied, "does indeed come first and reforms everything; but how is it that it is written of the Son of Man that He will endure much suffering and be held in contempt?

v13 Yet I tell you that not only has Elijah come, but they have also done to him whatever they chose, as the Scriptures say about him."

v14 As they came to rejoin the disciples, they saw an immense crowd surrounding them and a party of Scribes disputing with them.

v15 Immediately the whole multitude on beholding Him were astonished and awe-struck, and yet they ran forward and greeted Him.

v16 "What is the subject you are discussing?" He asked them.

v17 "Rabbi," answered one of the crowd, "I have brought you my son. He has a dumb spirit in him;

v18 and wherever it comes upon him, it dashes him to the ground, and he foams at the mouth and grinds his teeth, and he is pining away. I begged your disciples to expel it, but they had not the power."

v19 "O unbelieving generation!" replied Jesus; "how long must I be with you? how long must I have patience with you? Bring the boy to me."

v20 So they brought him to Jesus. And the spirit, when he saw Jesus, immediately threw the youth into convulsions, so that he fell on the ground and rolled about, foaming at the mouth.

v21 Then Jesus asked the father, "How long has he been like this?" "From early childhood," he said;

v22 "and often it has thrown him into the fire or into pools of water to destroy him. But, if you possibly can, have pity on us and help us."

v23 "'If I possibly can!'" replied Jesus; "why, everything is possible to him who believes."

v24 Immediately the father cried out, "I do believe: strengthen my weak faith."

v25 Then Jesus, seeing that an increasing crowd was running towards Him, rebuked the foul spirit, and said to it, "Dumb and deaf spirit, *I* command you, come out of him and never enter into him again."

v26 So with a loud cry he threw the boy into fit after fit, and came out. The boy looked as if he were dead, so that most of them said he was dead;

v27 but Jesus took his hand and raised him up, and he stood on his feet.

v28 After the return of Jesus to the house His disciples asked Him privately, "How is it that we could not expel the spirit?"

v29 "An evil spirit of this kind," He answered, "can only be driven out by prayer."

v30 Departing thence they passed through Galilee, and He was unwilling that any one should know it;

v31 for He was teaching His disciples, and telling them, "The Son of Man is to be betrayed into the hands of men, and they will put Him to death; and after being put to death, in three days He will rise to life again."

v32 They, however, did not understand what He meant, and were afraid to question Him.

v33 So they came to Capernaum; and when in the house He asked them, "What were you arguing about on the way?"

v34 But they remained silent; for on the way they had debated with one another who was the chief of them.

v35 Then sitting down He called the Twelve, and said to them, "If any one wishes to be first, he must be last of all and servant of all."

v36 And taking a young child He made him stand in their midst, then threw His arms round him and said,

v37 "Whoever for my sake receives one such young child as this, receives me; and whoever receives me, receives not so much me as Him who sent me."

v38 "Rabbi," said John to Him, "we saw a man making use of your name to expel demons, and we tried to hinder him, on the ground that he did not follow us."

v39 "You should not have tried to hinder him," replied Jesus, "for there is no one who will use my name to perform a miracle and be able the next minute to speak evil of me.

v40 He who is not against us is for us;

v41 and whoever gives you a cup of water to drink because you belong to Christ, I solemnly tell you that he will certainly not lose his reward.

v42 "And whoever shall occasion the fall of one of these little ones who believe, he would be better off if, with a millstone round his neck, he were lying at the bottom of the sea.

v43 If your hand should cause you to sin, cut it off: it would be better for you to enter into Life maimed, than remain in possession of both your hands and go away into Gehenna, into the fire which cannot be put out.

v44 []

v45 Or if your foot should cause you to sin, cut it off: it would be better for you to enter into Life crippled, than remain in possession of both your feet and be thrown into Gehenna.

v46 []

v47 Or if your eye should cause you to sin, tear it out. It would be better for you to enter into the Kingdom of God half-blind than remain in possession of two eyes and be thrown into Gehenna,

v48 where <their worm does not die and the fire does not go out.>

v49 Every one, however, will be salted with fire.

v50 Salt is a good thing, but if the salt should become tasteless, what will you use to give it saltness? Have salt within you and live at peace with one another."
