# Proverbs 27 (American Standard Version)

[[Bible Versions/ASV/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 26|← Proverbs 26]] | [[Bible Versions/ASV/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs]] | [[Bible Versions/ASV/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 28|Proverbs 28 →]]

***

v1 Boast not thyself of tomorrow; For thou knowest not what a day may bring forth.

v2 Let another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth; A stranger, and not thine own lips.

v3 A stone is heavy, and the sand weighty; But a fool's vexation is heavier than they both.

v4 Wrath is cruel, and anger is overwhelming; But who is able to stand before jealousy?

v5 Better is open rebuke Than love that is hidden.

v6 Faithful are the wounds of a friend; But the kisses of an enemy are profuse.

v7 The full soul loatheth a honeycomb; But to the hungry soul every bitter thing is sweet.

v8 As a bird that wandereth from her nest, So is a man that wandereth from his place.

v9 Oil and perfume rejoice the heart; So doth the sweetness of a man's friend [that cometh] of hearty    counsel.

v10 Thine own friend, and thy father's friend, forsake not; And go not to thy brother's house in the day of thy calamity: Better is a neighbor that is near than a brother far off.

v11 My son, be wise, and make my heart glad, That I may answer him that reproacheth me.

v12 A prudent man seeth the evil, [and] hideth himself; [But] the simple pass on, [and] suffer for it.

v13 Take his garment that is surety for a stranger; And hold him in pledge [that is surety] for a foreign woman.

v14 He that blesseth his friend with a loud voice, rising    early in the morning, It shall be counted a curse to him.

v15 A continual dropping in a very rainy day And a contentious woman are alike:

v16 He that would restrain her restraineth the wind; And his right hand encountereth oil.

v17 Iron sharpeneth iron; So a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.

v18 Whoso keepeth the fig-tree shall eat the fruit thereof; And he that regardeth his master shall be honored.

v19 As in water face [answereth] to face, So the heart of man to man.

v20 Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied; And the eyes of man are never satisfied.

v21 The refining pot is for silver, and the furnace for gold; And a man is [tried] by his praise.

v22 Though thou shouldest bray a fool in a mortar with a    pestle along with bruised grain, Yet will not his foolishness depart from him.

v23 Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, [And] look well to thy herds:

v24 For riches are not for ever: And doth the crown endure unto all generations?

v25 The hay is carried, and the tender grass showeth itself, And the herbs of the mountains are gathered in.

v26 The lambs are for thy clothing, And the goats are the price of the field;

v27 And [there will be] goats' milk enough for thy food, for    the food of thy household, And maintenance for thy maidens.
