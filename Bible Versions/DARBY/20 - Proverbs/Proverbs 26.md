# Proverbs 26 (Darby Translation)

[[Bible Versions/DARBY/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 25|← Proverbs 25]] | [[Bible Versions/DARBY/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs]] | [[Bible Versions/DARBY/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 27|Proverbs 27 →]]

***

v1 As snow in summer, and as rain in harvest, so honour beseemeth not a fool.

v2 As the sparrow for flitting about, as the swallow for flying, so a curse undeserved shall not come.

v3 A whip for the horse, a bridle for the ass, and a rod for the back of fools.

v4 Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou also be like unto him.

v5 Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own eyes.

v6 He that sendeth a message by the hand of a fool cutteth off [his own] feet, [and] drinketh damage.

v7 The legs of the lame hang loose; so is a proverb in the mouth of fools.

v8 As a bag of gems in a stoneheap, so is he that giveth honour to a fool.

v9 [As] a thorn goeth up into the hand of a drunkard, so is a proverb in the mouth of fools.

v10 A master roughly worketh every one: he both hireth the fool and hireth passers-by.

v11 As a dog turneth back to its vomit, [so] a fool repeateth his folly.

v12 Hast thou seen a man wise in his own eyes? There is more hope of a fool than of him.

v13 The sluggard saith, There is a fierce lion in the way; a lion is in the midst of the streets!

v14 [As] the door turneth upon its hinges, so the sluggard upon his bed.

v15 The sluggard burieth his hand in the dish: it wearieth him to bring it again to his mouth.

v16 A sluggard is wiser in his own eyes than seven [men] that answer discreetly.

v17 He that passing by vexeth himself with strife belonging not to him, is [like] one that taketh a dog by the ears.

v18 As a madman who casteth firebrands, arrows, and death,

v19 so is a man that deceiveth his neighbour, and saith, Am I not in sport?

v20 Where no wood is, the fire goeth out; and where there is no talebearer, the contention ceaseth.

v21 [As] coals for hot coals, and wood for fire, so is a contentious man to inflame strife.

v22 The words of a talebearer are as dainty morsels, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly.

v23 Ardent lips, and a wicked heart, are [as] an earthen vessel overlaid with silver dross.

v24 He that hateth dissembleth with his lips, but he layeth up deceit within him:

v25 when his voice is gracious, believe him not, for there are seven abominations in his heart.

v26 Though [his] hatred is covered by dissimulation, his wickedness shall be made manifest in the congregation.

v27 Whoso diggeth a pit shall fall therein; and he that rolleth a stone, it shall return upon him.

v28 A lying tongue hateth those that are injured by it, and a flattering mouth worketh ruin.
