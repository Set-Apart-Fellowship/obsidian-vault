# Proverbs 27 (Darby Translation)

[[Bible Versions/DARBY/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 26|← Proverbs 26]] | [[Bible Versions/DARBY/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs]] | [[Bible Versions/DARBY/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 28|Proverbs 28 →]]

***

v1 Boast not thyself of to-morrow, for thou knowest not what a day will bring forth.

v2 Let another praise thee, and not thine own mouth; a stranger, and not thine own lips.

v3 A stone is heavy, and the sand weighty; but a fool's vexation is heavier than them both.

v4 Fury is cruel, and anger is outrageous; but who is able to stand before jealousy?

v5 Open rebuke is better than hidden love.

v6 Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are profuse.

v7 The full soul trampleth on a honeycomb; but to the hungry soul every bitter thing is sweet.

v8 As a bird that wandereth from her nest, so is a man that wandereth from his place.

v9 Ointment and perfume rejoice the heart; and the sweetness of one's friend is [the fruit] of hearty counsel.

v10 Thine own friend, and thy father's friend, forsake not; and go not into thy brother's house in the day of thy calamity: better is a neighbour that is near than a brother far off.

v11 Be wise, my son, and make my heart glad, that I may have wherewith to answer him that reproacheth me.

v12 A prudent [man] seeth the evil, [and] hideth himself; the simple pass on, [and] are punished.

v13 Take his garment that is become surety [for] another, and hold him in pledge for a strange woman.

v14 He that blesseth his friend with a loud voice, rising early in the morning, it shall be reckoned a curse to him.

v15 A continual dropping on a very rainy day and a contentious woman are alike:

v16 whosoever will restrain her restraineth the wind, and his right hand encountereth oil.

v17 Iron is sharpened by iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.

v18 Whoso keepeth the fig-tree shall eat the fruit thereof; and he that guardeth his master shall be honoured.

v19 As [in] water face [answereth] to face, so the heart of man to man.

v20 Sheol and destruction are insatiable; so the eyes of man are never satisfied.

v21 The fining-pot is for silver, and the furnace for gold; so let a man be to the mouth that praiseth him.

v22 If thou shouldest bray a fool in a mortar among wheat with a pestle, yet will not his folly depart from him.

v23 Be well acquainted with the appearance of thy flocks; look well to thy herds:

v24 for wealth is not for ever; and doth the crown [endure] from generation to generation?

v25 The hay is removed, and the tender grass sheweth itself, and herbs of the mountains are gathered in.

v26 The lambs are for thy clothing, and the goats are the price of a field;

v27 and there is goats' milk enough for thy food, for the food of thy household, and sustenance for thy maidens.
