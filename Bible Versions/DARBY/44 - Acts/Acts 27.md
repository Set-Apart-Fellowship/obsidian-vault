# Acts 27 (Darby Translation)

[[Bible Versions/DARBY/44 - Acts/Acts 26|← Acts 26]] | [[Bible Versions/DARBY/44 - Acts/Acts]] | [[Bible Versions/DARBY/44 - Acts/Acts 28|Acts 28 →]]

***

v1 But when it had been determined that we should sail to Italy, they delivered up Paul and certain other prisoners to a centurion, by name Julius, of Augustus' company.

v2 And going on board a ship of Adramyttium about to navigate by the places along Asia, we set sail, Aristarchus, a Macedonian of Thessalonica, being with us.

v3 And the next day we arrived at Sidon. And Julius treated Paul kindly and suffered him to go to his friends and refresh himself.

v4 And setting sail thence we sailed under the lee of Cyprus, because the winds were contrary.

v5 And having sailed over the waters of Cilicia and Pamphylia we came to Myra in Lycia:

v6 and there the centurion having found a ship of Alexandria sailing to Italy, he made us go on board her.

v7 And sailing slowly for many days, and having with difficulty got abreast of Cnidus, the wind not suffering us, we sailed under the lee of Crete abreast of Salmone;

v8 and coasting it with difficulty we came to a certain place called Fair Havens, near to which was [the] city of Lasaea.

v9 And much time having now been spent, and navigation being already dangerous, because the fast also was already past, Paul counselled them,

v10 saying, Men, I perceive that the navigation will be with disaster and much loss, not only of the cargo and the ship, but also of our lives.

v11 But the centurion believed rather the helmsman and the shipowner than what was said by Paul.

v12 And the harbour being ill adapted to winter in, the most counselled to set sail thence, if perhaps they might reach Phoenice to winter in, a port of Crete looking north-east and south-east.

v13 And [the] south wind blowing gently, supposing that they had gained their object, having weighed anchor they sailed close in shore along Crete.

v14 But not long after there came down it a hurricane called Euroclydon.

v15 And the ship being caught and driven, and not able to bring her head to the wind, letting her go we were driven [before it].

v16 But running under the lee of a certain island called Clauda, we were with difficulty able to make ourselves masters of the boat;

v17 which having hoisted up, they used helps, frapping the ship; and fearing lest they should run into Syrtis and run aground, and having lowered the gear they were so driven.

v18 But the storm being extremely violent on us, on the next day they threw cargo overboard,

v19 and on the third day with their own hands they cast away the ship furniture.

v20 And neither sun nor stars appearing for many days, and no small storm lying on us, in the end all hope of our being saved was taken away.

v21 And when they had been a long while without taking food, Paul then standing up in the midst of them said, Ye ought, O men, to have hearkened to me, and not have made sail from Crete and have gained this disaster and loss.

v22 And now I exhort you to be of good courage, for there shall be no loss at all of life of [any] of you, only of the ship.

v23 For an angel of the God, whose I am and whom I serve, stood by me this night,

v24 saying, Fear not, Paul; thou must stand before Caesar; and behold, God has granted to thee all those that sail with thee.

v25 Wherefore be of good courage, men, for I believe God that thus it shall be, as it has been said to me.

v26 But we must be cast ashore on a certain island.

v27 And when the fourteenth night was come, we being driven about in Adria, towards the middle of the night the sailors supposed that some land neared them,

v28 and having sounded found twenty fathoms, and having gone a little farther and having again sounded they found fifteen fathoms;

v29 and fearing lest we should be cast on rocky places, casting four anchors out of the stern, they wished that day were come.

v30 But the sailors wishing to flee out of the ship, and having let down the boat into the sea under pretext of being about to carry out anchors from the prow,

v31 Paul said to the centurion and the soldiers, Unless these abook_ide in the ship *ye* cannot be saved.

v32 Then the soldiers cut away the ropes of the boat and let her fall.

v33 And while it was drawing on to daylight, Paul exhorted them all to partake of food, saying, Ye have passed the fourteenth day watching in expectation without taking food.

v34 Wherefore I exhort you to partake of food, for this has to do with your safety; for not a hair from the head of any one of you shall perish.

v35 And, having said these things and taken a loaf, he gave thanks to God before all, and having broken it began to eat.

v36 And all taking courage, themselves also took food.

v37 And we were in the ship, all the souls, two hundred and seventy-six.

v38 And having satisfied themselves with food, they lightened the ship, casting out the wheat into the sea.

v39 And when it was day they did not recognise the land; but they perceived a certain bay having a strand, on which they were minded, if they should be able, to run the ship ashore;

v40 and, having cast off the anchors, they left [them] in the sea, at the same time loosening the lashings of the rudders, and hoisting the foresail to the wind, they made for the strand.

v41 And falling into a place where two seas met they ran the ship aground, and the prow having stuck itself fast remained unmoved, but the stern was broken by the force of the waves.

v42 And [the] counsel of the soldiers was that they should kill the prisoners, lest any one should swim off and escape.

v43 But the centurion, desirous of saving Paul, hindered them of their purpose, and commanded those who were able to swim, casting themselves first [into the sea], to get out on land;

v44 and the rest, some on boards, some on some of the things [that came] from the ship; and thus it came to pass that all got safe to land.
