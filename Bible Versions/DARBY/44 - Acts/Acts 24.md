# Acts 24 (Darby Translation)

[[Bible Versions/DARBY/44 - Acts/Acts 23|← Acts 23]] | [[Bible Versions/DARBY/44 - Acts/Acts]] | [[Bible Versions/DARBY/44 - Acts/Acts 25|Acts 25 →]]

***

v1 And after five days came down the high priest Ananias, with the elders, and a certain orator called Tertullus, and laid their informations against Paul before the governor.

v2 And he having been called, Tertullus began to accuse, saying, Seeing we enjoy great peace through thee, and that excellent measures are executed for this nation by thy forethought,

v3 we receive [it] always and everywhere, most excellent Felix, with all thankfulness.

v4 But that I may not too much intrude on thy time, I beseech thee to hear us briefly in thy kindness.

v5 For finding this man a pest, and moving sedition among all the Jews throughout the world, and a leader of the sect of the Nazaraeans;

v6 who also attempted to profane the temple; whom we also had seized, [and would have judged according to our law;

v7 but Lysias, the chiliarch, coming up, took [him] away with great force out of our hands,

v8 having commanded his accusers to come to thee;] of whom thou canst thyself, in examining [him], know the certainty of all these things of which we accuse him.

v9 And the Jews also joined in pressing the matter against [Paul], saying that these things were so.

v10 But Paul, the governor having beckoned to him to speak, answered, Knowing that for many years thou hast been judge to this nation, I answer readily as to the things which concern myself.

v11 As thou mayest know that there are not more than twelve days since I went up to worship at Jerusalem,

v12 and neither in the temple did they find me discoursing to any one, or making any tumultuous gathering together of the crowd, nor in the synagogues, nor in the city;

v13 neither can they make good the things of which they now accuse me.

v14 But this I avow to thee, that in the way which they call sect, so I serve my fathers' God, believing all things which are written throughout the law, and in the prophets;

v15 having hope towards God, which they themselves also receive, that there is to be a resurrection both of just and unjust.

v16 For this cause I also exercise [myself] to have in everything a conscience without offence towards God and men.

v17 And after a lapse of many years I arrived, bringing alms to my nation, and offerings.

v18 Whereupon they found me purified in the temple, with neither crowd nor tumult. But it was certain Jews from Asia,

v19 who ought to appear before thee and accuse, if they have anything against me;

v20 or let these themselves say what wrong they found in me when I stood before the council,

v21 [other] than concerning this one voice which I cried standing amongst them: I am judged this day by you touching [the] resurrection of [the] dead.

v22 And Felix, knowing accurately the things concerning the way, adjourned them, saying, When Lysias the chiliarch is come down I will determine your affair;

v23 ordering the centurion to keep him, and that he should have freedom, and to hinder none of his friends to minister to him.

v24 And after certain days, Felix having arrived with Drusilla his wife, who was a Jewess, he sent for Paul and heard him concerning the faith in Christ.

v25 And as he reasoned concerning righteousness, and temperance, and the judgment about to come, Felix, being filled with fear, answered, Go for the present, and when I get an opportunity I will send for thee;

v26 hoping at the same time that money would be given him by Paul: wherefore also he sent for him the oftener and communed with him.

v27 But when two years were completed, Felix was relieved by Porcius Festus as his successor; and Felix, desirous to oblige the Jews, to acquire their favour, left Paul bound.
