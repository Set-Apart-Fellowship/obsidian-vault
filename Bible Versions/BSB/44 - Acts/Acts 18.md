# Acts 18 (Berean Standard Bible)
[[Bible Versions/BSB/44 - Acts/Acts 17|← Acts 17]] | [[Bible Versions/BSB/44 - Acts/Acts]] | [[Bible Versions/BSB/44 - Acts/Acts 19|Acts 19 →]]
***
v1 After this, Paul left Athens and went to Corinth.
v2 There he found a Jew named Aquila, a native of Pontus, who had recently come from Italy with his wife Priscilla because Claudius had ordered all the Jews to leave Rome. Paul went to visit them,
v3 and he stayed and worked with them because they were tentmakers by trade, just as he was.
v4 Every Sabbath he reasoned in the synagogue, trying to persuade Jews and Greeks alike.
v5 And when Silas and Timothy came down from Macedonia, Paul devoted himself fully to the word, testifying to the Jews that Jesus is the Christ.
v6 But when they opposed and insulted him, he shook out his garments and told them, “Your blood be on your own heads! I am innocent of it. From now on I will go to the Gentiles.”
v7 So Paul left the synagogue and went next door to the house of Titus Justus, a worshiper of God.
v8 Crispus, the synagogue leader, and his whole household believed in the Lord. And many of the Corinthians who heard the message believed and were baptized.
v9 One night the Lord spoke to Paul in a vision: “Do not be afraid; keep on speaking; do not be silent.
v10 For I am with you and no one will lay a hand on you, because I have many people in this city.”
v11 So Paul stayed for a year and a half, teaching the word of God among the Corinthians.
v12 While Gallio was proconsul of Achaia, the Jews coordinated an attack on Paul and brought him before the judgment seat.
v13 “This man is persuading the people to worship God in ways contrary to the law,” they said.
v14 But just as Paul was about to speak, Gallio told the Jews, “If this matter involved a wrongdoing or vicious crime, O Jews, it would be reasonable for me to hear your complaint.
v15 But since it is a dispute about words and names and your own law, settle it yourselves. I refuse to be a judge of such things.”
v16 And he drove them away from the judgment seat.
v17 At this, the crowd seized Sosthenes the synagogue leader and beat him in front of the judgment seat. But none of this was of concern to Gallio.
v18 Paul remained in Corinth for quite some time before saying goodbye to the brothers. He had his head shaved in Cenchrea to keep a vow he had made, and then he sailed for Syria, accompanied by Priscilla and Aquila.
v19 When they reached Ephesus, Paul left Priscilla and Aquila. He himself went into the synagogue there and reasoned with the Jews.
v20 When they asked him to stay for a while longer, he declined.
v21 But as he left, he said, “I will come back to you if God is willing.” And he set sail from Ephesus.
v22 When Paul had landed at Caesarea, he went up and greeted the church at Jerusalem. Then he went down to Antioch.
v23 After Paul had spent some time in Antioch, he traveled from place to place throughout the region of Galatia and Phrygia, strengthening all the disciples.
v24 Meanwhile a Jew named Apollos, a native of Alexandria, came to Ephesus. He was an eloquent man, well versed in the Scriptures.
v25 He had been instructed in the way of the Lord and was fervent in spirit. He spoke and taught accurately about Jesus, though he knew only the baptism of John.
v26 And he began to speak boldly in the synagogue. When Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and explained to him the way of God more accurately.
v27 When Apollos resolved to cross over to Achaia, the brothers encouraged him and wrote to the disciples there to welcome him. On his arrival, he was a great help to those who by grace had believed.
v28 For he powerfully refuted the Jews in public debate, proving from the Scriptures that Jesus is the Christ.
