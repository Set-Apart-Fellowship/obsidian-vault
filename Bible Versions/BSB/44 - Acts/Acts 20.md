# Acts 20 (Berean Standard Bible)
[[Bible Versions/BSB/44 - Acts/Acts 19|← Acts 19]] | [[Bible Versions/BSB/44 - Acts/Acts]] | [[Bible Versions/BSB/44 - Acts/Acts 21|Acts 21 →]]
***
v1 When the uproar had ended, Paul sent for the disciples. And after encouraging them, he said goodbye to them and left for Macedonia.
v2 After traveling through that area and speaking many words of encouragement, he arrived in Greece,
v3 where he stayed three months. And when the Jews formed a plot against him as he was about to sail for Syria, he decided to go back through Macedonia.
v4 Paul was accompanied by Sopater son of Pyrrhus from Berea, Aristarchus and Secundus from Thessalonica, Gaius from Derbe, Timothy, and Tychicus and Trophimus from the province of Asia.
v5 These men went on ahead and waited for us in Troas.
v6 And after the Feast of Unleavened Bread, we sailed from Philippi, and five days later we rejoined them in Troas, where we stayed seven days.
v7 On the first day of the week we came together to break bread. Since Paul was ready to leave the next day, he talked to them and kept on speaking until midnight.
v8 Now there were many lamps in the upper room where we were gathered.
v9 And a certain young man named Eutychus, seated in the window, was sinking into a deep sleep as Paul talked on and on. When he was sound asleep, he fell from the third story and was picked up dead.
v10 But Paul went down, threw himself on the young man, and embraced him. “Do not be alarmed!” he said. “He is still alive!”
v11 Then Paul went back upstairs, broke bread, and ate. And after speaking until daybreak, he departed.
v12 And the people were greatly relieved to take the boy home alive.
v13 We went on ahead to the ship and sailed to Assos, where we were to take Paul aboard. He had arranged this because he was going there on foot.
v14 And when he met us at Assos, we took him aboard and went on to Mitylene.
v15 Sailing on from there, we arrived the next day opposite Chios. The day after that we arrived at Samos, and on the following day we came to Miletus.
v16 Paul had decided to sail past Ephesus to avoid spending time in the province of Asia, because he was in a hurry to reach Jerusalem, if possible, by the day of Pentecost.
v17 From Miletus, Paul sent to Ephesus for the elders of the church.
v18 When they came to him, he said, “You know how I lived the whole time I was with you, from the first day I arrived in the province of Asia.
v19 I served the Lord with great humility and with tears, especially in the trials that came upon me through the plots of the Jews.
v20 I did not shrink back from declaring anything that was helpful to you as I taught you publicly and from house to house,
v21 testifying to Jews and Greeks alike about repentance to God and faith in our Lord Jesus Christ.
v22 And now, compelled by the Spirit, I am going to Jerusalem, not knowing what will happen to me there.
v23 I only know that in town after town the Holy Spirit warns me that chains and afflictions await me.
v24 But I consider my life of no value to me, if only I may finish my course and complete the ministry I have received from the Lord Jesus—the ministry of testifying to the good news of God’s grace.
v25 Now I know that none of you among whom I have preached the kingdom will see my face again.
v26 Therefore I testify to you this day that I am innocent of the blood of all men.
v27 For I did not shrink back from declaring to you the whole will of God.
v28 Keep watch over yourselves and the entire flock of which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers. Be shepherds of the church of God, which He purchased with His own blood.
v29 I know that after my departure, savage wolves will come in among you and will not spare the flock.
v30 Even from your own number, men will rise up and distort the truth to draw away disciples after them.
v31 Therefore be alert and remember that for three years I never stopped warning each of you night and day with tears.
v32 And now I commit you to God and to the word of His grace, which can build you up and give you an inheritance among all who are sanctified.
v33 I have not coveted anyone’s silver or gold or clothing.
v34 You yourselves know that these hands of mine have ministered to my own needs and those of my companions.
v35 In everything, I showed you that by this kind of hard work we must help the weak, remembering the words of the Lord Jesus Himself: ‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’”
v36 When Paul had said this, he knelt down with all of them and prayed.
v37 They all wept openly as they embraced Paul and kissed him.
v38 They were especially grieved by his statement that they would never see his face again. Then they accompanied him to the ship.
