v1 My son, if thou become surety for thy friend, thou shalt deliver thine hand to an enemy.
v2 For a man's own lips become a strong snare to him, and he is caught with the lips of his own mouth.
v3 My son, do what I command thee, and deliver thyself; for on thy friend's account thou art come into the power of evil men : faint not, but stir up even thy friend for whom thou art become surety.
v4 Give not sleep to thine eyes, nor slumber with thine eyelids;
v5 that thou mayest deliver thyself as a doe out of the toils, and as a bird out of a snare.
v6 Go to the ant, O sluggard; and see, and emulate his ways, and become wiser than he.
v7 For whereas he has no husbandry, nor any one to compel him, and is under no master,
v8 he prepares food for himself in the summer, and lays by abundant store in harvest.
v9 How long wilt thou lie, O sluggard? and when wilt thou awake out of sleep?
v10 Thou sleepest a little, and thou restest a little, and thou slumberest a short time , and thou foldest thine arms over thy breast a little.
v11 Then poverty comes upon thee as an evil traveller, and want as a swift courier:
v12 A foolish man and a transgressor goes in ways that are not good.
v13 And the same winks with the eye, and makes a sign with his foot, and teaches with the beckonings of his fingers.
v14 His perverse heart devises evils: at all times such a one causes troubles to a city.
v15 Therefore his destruction shall come suddenly; overthrow and irretrievable ruin.
v16 For he rejoices in all things which God hates, and he is ruined by reason of impurity of soul.
v17 The eye of the haughty, a tongue unjust, hands shedding the blood of the just;
v18 and a heart devising evil thoughts, and feet hastening to do evil,— are hateful to God .
v19 An unjust witness kindles falsehoods, and brings on quarrels between brethren.
v20 My son, keep the laws of thy father, and reject not the ordinances of thy mother:
v21 but bind them upon thy soul continually, and hang them as a chain about thy neck.
v22 Whensoever thou walkest, lead this along and let it be with thee; and when thou sleepest let it keep thee; that it may talk with thee when thou wakest.
v23 For the commandment of the law is a lamp and a light; a way of life; reproof also and correction:
v24 to keep thee continually from a married woman, and from the calumny of a strange tongue.
v25 Let not the desire of beauty overcome thee, neither be thou caught by thine eyes, neither be captivated with her eyelids.
v26 For the value of a harlot is as much as of one loaf; and a woman hunts for the precious souls of men.
v27 Shall any one bind fire in his bosom, and not burn his garments?
v28 or will any one walk on coals of fire, and not burn his feet?
v29 So is he that goes in to a married woman; he shall not be held guiltless, neither any one that touches her.
v30 It is not to be wondered at if one should be taken stealing, for he steals that when hungry he may satisfy his soul:
v31 but if he should be taken, he shall repay sevenfold, and shall deliver himself by giving all his goods.
v32 But the adulterer through want of sense procures destruction to his soul.
v33 He endures both pain and disgrace, and his reproach shall never be wiped off.
v34 For the soul of her husband is full of jealousy: he will not spare in the day of vengeance.
v35 He will not forego his enmity for any ransom: neither will he be reconciled for many gifts.
