# Acts 27 (Young's Literal Translation)

[[Bible Versions/YLT/44 - Acts/Acts 26|← Acts 26]] | [[Bible Versions/YLT/44 - Acts/Acts]] | [[Bible Versions/YLT/44 - Acts/Acts 28|Acts 28 →]]

***

v1 And when our sailing to Italy was determined, they were delivering up both Paul and certain others, prisoners, to a centurion, by name Julius, of the band of Sebastus,

v2 and having embarked in a ship of Adramyttium, we, being about to sail by the coasts of Asia, did set sail, there being with us Aristarchus, a Macedonian of Thessalonica,

v3 on the next [day] also we touched at Sidon, and Julius, courteously treating Paul, did permit [him], having gone on unto friends, to receive [their] care.

v4 And thence, having set sail, we sailed under Cyprus, because of the winds being contrary,

v5 and having sailed over the sea over-against Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came to Myria of Lycia,

v6 and there the centurion having found a ship of Alexandria, sailing to Italy, did put us into it,

v7 and having sailed slowly many days, and with difficulty coming over-against Cnidus, the wind not suffering us, we sailed under Crete, over-against Salmone,

v8 and hardly passing it, we came to a certain place called 'Fair Havens,' nigh to which was the city [of] Lasaea.

v9 And much time being spent, and the sailing being now dangerous -- because of the fast also being already past -- Paul was admonishing,

v10 saying to them, 'Men, I perceive that with hurt, and much damage, not only of the lading and of the ship, but also of our lives -- the voyage is about to be;'

v11 but the centurion to the pilot and to the shipowner gave credence more than to the things spoken by Paul;

v12 and the haven being incommodious to winter in, the more part gave counsel to sail thence also, if by any means they might be able, having attained to Phenice, [there] to winter, [which is] a haven of Crete, looking to the south-west and north-west,

v13 and a south wind blowing softly, having thought they had obtained [their] purpose, having lifted anchor, they sailed close by Crete,

v14 and not long after there arose against it a tempestuous wind, that is called Euroclydon,

v15 and the ship being caught, and not being able to bear up against the wind, having given [her] up, we were borne on,

v16 and having run under a certain little isle, called Clauda, we were hardly able to become masters of the boat,

v17 which having taken up, they were using helps, undergirding the ship, and fearing lest they may fall on the quicksand, having let down the mast -- so were borne on.

v18 And we, being exceedingly tempest-tossed, the succeeding [day] they were making a clearing,

v19 and on the third [day] with our own hands the tackling of the ship we cast out,

v20 and neither sun nor stars appearing for more days, and not a little tempest lying upon us, thenceforth all hope was taken away of our being saved.

v21 And there having been long fasting, then Paul having stood in the midst of them, said, 'It behoved [you], indeed, O men -- having hearkened to me -- not to set sail from Crete, and to save this hurt and damage;

v22 and now I exhort you to be of good cheer, for there shall be no loss of life among you -- but of the ship;

v23 for there stood by me this night a messenger of God -- whose I am, and whom I serve --

v24 saying, Be not afraid Paul; before Caesar it behoveth thee to stand; and, lo, God hath granted to thee all those sailing with thee;

v25 wherefore be of good cheer, men! for I believe God, that so it shall be, even as it hath been spoken to me,

v26 and on a certain island it behoveth us to be cast.'

v27 And when the fourteenth night came -- we being borne up and down in the Adria -- toward the middle of the night the sailors were supposing that some country drew nigh to them;

v28 and having sounded they found twenty fathoms, and having gone a little farther, and again having sounded, they found fifteen fathoms,

v29 and fearing lest on rough places we may fall, out of the stern having cast four anchors, they were wishing day to come.

v30 And the sailors seeking to flee out of the ship, and having let down the boat to the sea, in pretence as [if] out of the foreship they are about to cast anchors,

v31 Paul said to the centurion and to the soldiers, 'If these do not remain in the ship -- ye are not able to be saved;'

v32 then the soldiers did cut off the ropes of the boat, and suffered it to fall off.

v33 And till the day was about to be, Paul was calling upon all to partake of nourishment, saying, 'Fourteen days to-day, waiting, ye continue fasting, having taken nothing,

v34 wherefore I call upon you to take nourishment, for this is for your safety, for of not one of you shall a hair from the head fall;'

v35 and having said these things, and having taken bread, he gave thanks to God before all, and having broken [it], he began to eat;

v36 and all having become of good cheer, themselves also took food,

v37 (and we were -- all the souls in the ship -- two hundred, seventy and six),

v38 and having eaten sufficient nourishment, they were lightening the ship, casting forth the wheat into the sea.

v39 And when the day came, they were not discerning the land, but a certain creek were perceiving having a beach, into which they took counsel, if possible, to thrust forward the ship,

v40 and the anchors having taken up, they were committing [it] to the sea, at the same time -- having loosed the bands of the rudders, and having hoisted up the mainsail to the wind -- they were making for the shore,

v41 and having fallen into a place of two seas, they ran the ship aground, and the fore-part, indeed, having stuck fast, did remain immoveable, but the hinder-part was broken by the violence of the waves.

v42 And the soldiers' counsel was that they should kill the prisoners, lest any one having swam out should escape,

v43 but the centurion, wishing to save Paul, hindered them from the counsel, and did command those able to swim, having cast themselves out first -- to get unto the land,

v44 and the rest, some indeed upon boards, and some upon certain things of the ship; and thus it came to pass that all came safe unto the land.
