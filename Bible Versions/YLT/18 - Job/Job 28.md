# Job 28 (Young's Literal Translation)

[[Bible Versions/YLT/18 - Job/Job 27|← Job 27]] | [[Bible Versions/YLT/18 - Job/Job]] | [[Bible Versions/YLT/18 - Job/Job 29|Job 29 →]]

***

v1 Surely there is for silver a source, And a place for the gold they refine;

v2 Iron from the dust is taken, And [from] the firm stone brass.

v3 An end hath he set to darkness, And to all perfection he is searching, A stone of darkness and death-shade.

v4 A stream hath broken out from a sojourner, Those forgotten of the foot, They were low, from man they wandered.

v5 The earth! from it cometh forth bread, And its under-part is turned like fire.

v6 A place of the sapphire [are] its stones, And it hath dust of gold.

v7 A path -- not known it hath a ravenous fowl, Nor scorched it hath an eye of the kite,

v8 Nor trodden it have the sons of pride, Not passed over it hath the fierce lion.

v9 Against the flint he sent forth his hand, He overturned from the root mountains.

v10 Among rocks, brooks he hath cleaved, And every precious thing hath his eye seen.

v11 From overflowing floods he hath bound, And the hidden thing bringeth out [to] light.

v12 And the wisdom -- whence is it found? And where [is] this, the place of understanding?

v13 Man hath not known its arrangement, Nor is it found in the land of the living.

v14 The deep hath said, 'It [is] not in me,' And the sea hath said, 'It is not with me.'

v15 Gold is not given for it, Nor is silver weighed -- its price.

v16 It is not valued with pure gold of Ophir, With precious onyx and sapphire,

v17 Not equal it do gold and crystal, Nor [is] its exchange a vessel of fine gold.

v18 Corals and pearl are not remembered, The acquisition of wisdom [is] above rubies.

v19 Not equal it doth the topaz of Cush, With pure gold it is not valued.

v20 And the wisdom -- whence doth it come? And where [is] this, the place of understanding?

v21 It hath been hid from the eyes of all living. And from the fowl of the heavens It hath been hidden.

v22 Destruction and death have said: 'With our ears we have heard its fame.'

v23 God hath understood its way, And He hath known its place.

v24 For He to the ends of the earth doth look, Under the whole heavens He doth see,

v25 To make for the wind a weight, And the waters He meted out in measure.

v26 In His making for the rain a limit, And a way for the brightness of the voices,

v27 Then He hath seen and declareth it, He hath prepared it, and also searched it out,

v28 And He saith to man: -- 'Lo, fear of the Lord, that [is] wisdom, And to turn from evil [is] understanding.'
