# Job 41 (Young's Literal Translation)

[[Bible Versions/YLT/18 - Job/Job 40|← Job 40]] | [[Bible Versions/YLT/18 - Job/Job]] | [[Bible Versions/YLT/18 - Job/Job 42|Job 42 →]]

***

v1 Dost thou draw leviathan with an angle? And with a rope thou lettest down -- his tongue?

v2 Dost thou put a reed in his nose? And with a thorn pierce his jaw?

v3 Doth he multiply unto thee supplications? Doth he speak unto thee tender things?

v4 Doth he make a covenant with thee? Dost thou take him for a servant age-during?

v5 Dost thou play with him as a bird? And dost thou bind him for thy damsels?

v6 (Feast upon him do companions, They divide him among the merchants!)

v7 Dost thou fill with barbed irons his skin? And with fish-spears his head?

v8 Place on him thy hand, Remember the battle -- do not add!

v9 Lo, the hope of him is found a liar, Also at his appearance is not one cast down?

v10 None so fierce that he doth awake him, And who [is] he before Me stationeth himself?

v11 Who hath brought before Me and I repay? Under the whole heavens it [is] mine.

v12 I do not keep silent concerning his parts, And the matter of might, And the grace of his arrangement.

v13 Who hath uncovered the face of his clothing? Within his double bridle who doth enter?

v14 The doors of his face who hath opened? Round about his teeth [are] terrible.

v15 A pride -- strong ones of shields, Shut up -- a close seal.

v16 One unto another they draw nigh, And air doth not enter between them.

v17 One unto another they adhere, They stick together and are not separated.

v18 His sneezings cause light to shine, And his eyes [are] as the eyelids of the dawn.

v19 Out of his mouth do flames go, sparks of fire escape.

v20 Out of his nostrils goeth forth smoke, As a blown pot and reeds.

v21 His breath setteth coals on fire, And a flame from his mouth goeth forth.

v22 In his neck lodge doth strength, And before him doth grief exult.

v23 The flakes of his flesh have adhered -- Firm upon him -- it is not moved.

v24 His heart [is] firm as a stone, Yea, firm as the lower piece.

v25 From his rising are the mighty afraid, From breakings they keep themselves free.

v26 The sword of his overtaker standeth not, Spear -- dart -- and lance.

v27 He reckoneth iron as straw, brass as rotten wood.

v28 The son of the bow doth not cause him to flee, Turned by him into stubble are stones of the sling.

v29 As stubble have darts been reckoned, And he laugheth at the shaking of a javelin.

v30 Under him [are] sharp points of clay, He spreadeth gold on the mire.

v31 He causeth to boil as a pot the deep, The sea he maketh as a pot of ointment.

v32 After him he causeth a path to shine, One thinketh the deep to be hoary.

v33 There is not on the earth his like, That is made without terror.

v34 Every high thing he doth see, He [is] king over all sons of pride.
